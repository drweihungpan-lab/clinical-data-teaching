# Instructor answers and discussion guidance

Release after discussion if useful. Answers emphasize reasoning, not calculations.

## Session 1

1. **Biomarker versus events:** no. A marker response addresses the measured biological effect; additional evidence is needed for a claim about clinical events. Ask students to state the actual study question before generalizing.
2. **CRO oversight:** no. Transferring an activity does not make sponsor oversight disappear. Exact documented responsibilities depend on the arrangement.
3. **Mouse paper to first human:** no. A published animal result is a starting point, not permission. Accept any reasonable pair: a science gap (replication, whether the mouse model reflects human disease, formal safety studies, a starting dose, a human endpoint) and a business gap (funding, a company to carry it, market need, a manufactured clinical batch, regulatory and ethics permission, sites).

## Session 2

1. **Missing laboratory value:** no. Zero is a measured value, not a missing-value code. Investigate whether a valid result can be recovered, retain unresolved missingness, and apply the planned analysis approach.
2. **AI success rate:** no. Two deliberately selected programs supply neither a representative sample nor a meaningful industry denominator. A candidate-level drug trial also does not estimate the causal effect of AI on discovery productivity.

For the role scenario: the site/lab establishes what happened; the data manager coordinates queries; CRA/operations facilitate follow-up; the programmer implements agreed rules and communicates denominators; the biostatistician assesses implications. Escalate medical, governance, QA, or RA questions when relevant, not reflexively on every record.

## Session 3

1. **Coded means anonymous:** no. If linkage or other information makes people identifiable, pseudonymized data remain personal data. Involve the controller/privacy team rather than guessing.
2. **Security means clinical performance:** no. Managing information-security risks addresses a different question from whether the test supports its intended clinical purpose.

## Final integrative discussion

A strong answer names the clinical question and evidence level, assigns responsibilities rather than only software, and distinguishes all three contexts: real published trial, invented data example, and fictional diagnostic product.

- **Study interpretation:** the biostatistician helps assess whether the design and evidence support the conclusion. A biomarker signal is not automatically clinical benefit.
- **Missing measurement:** source investigation belongs with site/lab and data management; analysis implications belong with the statistician/programmer. Unresolved missingness cannot be hidden by creating values.
- **Research export:** the data scientist may formulate exploration or feasibility questions; governance/privacy and security collaborators assess appropriate use and access. Programming/data-management work makes the authorized export interpretable. Product/evidence questions may need RA and the diagnostic team.
- **Next development decision:** explain what is known, what remains uncertain, and what additional evidence is relevant. Do not declare a real legal obligation, device class, or drug-program outcome from the fictional scenario.

Five minutes is sufficient. Invite two or three short responses, then summarize. Do not grade students on memorizing acronyms or regulation numbers.

## Survey key

Pre-class items 3–5 and post-class items 2–4: **biostatistician; no; no**. Other survey items have no right answer. Record “unsure” separately from incorrect choices and report the response denominator.
