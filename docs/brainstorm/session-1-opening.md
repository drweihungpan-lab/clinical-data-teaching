# Session 1 opening — brainstorm notes

Working notes, not yet lesson content. Started 2026-11-25.

## The idea

Open Session 1 with a story instead of definitions. A standalone fictional scenario — **not** tied to SLN360.

> **"A perfect discovery."** Dr. Lin finds that blocking protein X stops disease Y in mice. Published in a top journal. Headlines: "cure found."
>
> **Ask the room:** Could we give this to patients next month? What's missing?

Students call out gaps (or pair up for 3–4 minutes). The instructor then sorts their answers into five groups.

## The five gaps

| Group | The question | Data question under it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Biology | Does the mouse model reflect the human disease? Will it replicate in another lab? | Small samples, inflated effects, results that may not repeat |
| 2. The product | Is there an actual drug that can be made consistently, at scale? | Batch-to-batch quality data (CMC) |
| 3. Safety | What does it do to the rest of the body? What dose is safe to try first in a person? | Toxicology and dose-exposure data across species |
| 4. Evidence in people | Who gets it, what is measured, compared to what? → the protocol | A mouse outcome is not a human endpoint |
| 5. People, permission, money | Regulators, ethics review, hospitals, funding, teams to run it | Who owns, checks, and pays for the data |

Closing line: **"The rest of today walks this road."** This leads into the existing protocol, phases, and industry slides.

Lean so far: full journey (all five groups equal), with one data question under each, since Session 1 also covers the industry.

## New direction: the operations road (added 2026-11-25)

Follow the discovery operationally: how it gets funded, who carries it (biotech → pharma), and everything that has to be ready and approved before the first person is dosed.

### Funding and who carries it

- **University lab:** grants fund the discovery. The tech-transfer office files a patent. *Teaching hook: publishing before filing can damage patentability.*
- **Spin-out biotech:** founded around the patent and licensed from the university. Seed and Series A venture money pays for turning a mechanism into a drug candidate.
- **Pharma:** partners, licenses, or acquires, usually once there is human data, sometimes earlier. It brings money, development experience, and scale.
- **Service providers along the way:** CROs (preclinical and clinical studies) and CDMOs (making the clinical batch).

### Preparation before first-in-human

- **Choose a candidate:** turn "blocking protein X" into one specific molecule that can be dosed.
- **Pharmacology and PK:** does it reach the target in animals, and how long does it last?
- **IND-enabling (GLP) safety studies:** toxicology, typically in two species for small molecules, plus safety pharmacology and genotoxicity. The ICH M3(R2) guideline sets what is needed before which trial. Biologics follow ICH S6.
- **Manufacturing:** a GMP clinical batch with quality testing, which forms the CMC section.
- **Documents:** investigator's brochure, first protocol, informed consent, starting-dose rationale.

### Approval before the trial can start

- **US:** optional pre-IND meeting with FDA → IND submission → 30 days to begin unless FDA places a clinical hold. IRB approval at each site.
- **EU:** clinical-trial application through CTIS under the Clinical Trials Regulation. Member states and ethics committees assess it. EMA does not "approve" the trial.
- Plus site selection, contracts, insurance, and trial registration.

*Verify each regulatory detail against primary sources before it goes on a slide.*

## Decision: two directions, option A (2026-11-25)

**Placement — option A (chosen):** the operations story merges into the industry block (minutes 60–80). The biotech/pharma/CRO/CMO roles are taught through Dr. Lin's journey instead of as a list.

**Two directions (user's request):** the gaps are split into a science side and a business/operations side.

| Science: "Is it real, and is it safe?" | Business & operations: "Who pays for it, and can it get there?" |
| --- | --- |
| Reproducibility: will another lab get the same result? | Funding: grant → patent → spin-out biotech → VC → pharma partner |
| Biology: does the mouse reflect human disease? | Market: unmet need, number of patients, competitors, would anyone pay |
| Safety: toxicology, first safe dose in humans | Manufacturing: who makes a consistent clinical batch (CDMO) |
| Evidence in people: what to measure, compared to what | Approval to start: IND / EU CTA, ethics, sites, CROs |

*Proposed (Claude's call, to confirm):* show the two-column slide in the opening. The science column is answered in the first half (foundations, phases). The business column pays off in the industry block. Manufacturing and approval-to-start sit on the operations side, fed by the science side's safety data.

**Market depth (decided):** light — unmet need, number of patients, competitors. No pricing/reimbursement.

**Students generate first (Claude's call):** pairs list gaps for ~3 min, then the instructor reveals the two-column slide and sorts their answers into it.

Industry block as a journey (each step introduces an organization type):

1. **From paper to company** — grant → patent → spin-out biotech → venture money; light market check. *(Biotech)*
2. **Getting ready for first-in-human** — pick a candidate, GLP safety studies, clinical batch. *(CRO for preclinical work, CDMO for manufacturing)*
3. **Permission to start** — IND + 30 days (US), CTA via CTIS (EU), ethics, sites. *(Regulators, RA)*
4. **Who carries it further** — pharma partners/licenses, often after early human data; links back to phases. *(Pharma)*
5. Existing "sites turn plans into observations" slide, trimmed.
