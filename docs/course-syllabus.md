# Course Syllabus: Data Processing in Clinical Trials & the Bio-Pharma Ecosystem

## Course Overview

**Total duration:** 6 hours  
**Format:** 3 sessions, 2 hours per session  
**Target audience:** Graduate students in Statistics and Data Science

## Course Objective

This intensive course bridges the gap between academic statistics and the global biopharmaceutical industry. Students will develop a practical understanding of:

- The drug and medical device development lifecycle
- The corporate ecosystem surrounding clinical development, including Pharma, Biotech, CROs, and CMOs
- Regulatory landscapes and data standards used in clinical research
- The evolving roles of Biostatisticians and Data Scientists in the era of artificial intelligence and digital health

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of the course, students should be able to:

1. Describe the objectives, data, and statistical characteristics of clinical trial Phases I–IV.
2. Explain how Pharma, Biotech, CRO, and CMO organizations contribute to the biopharma value chain.
3. Compare the responsibilities, methods, and tools used by Biostatisticians and Data Scientists.
4. Identify practical applications of AI, machine learning, real-world evidence, and digital health data in clinical development.
5. Recognize the roles of FDA, EMA, CDISC, and GxP requirements in regulated data workflows.

## Session Schedule

### Session 1: Drug Development Lifecycle & the Corporate Ecosystem

**Duration:** 2 hours

#### The Clinical Trial Pipeline

- Deconstruct Phases I–IV by examining their objectives, typical sample sizes, endpoints, and data collection requirements.
- Trace the progression from early safety and dose-finding studies through confirmatory trials and post-market surveillance.
- Discuss how the purpose and structure of clinical data change across the development lifecycle.

#### Mapping the Global Industry Ecosystem

- **Pharma:** Understand the operating model, product portfolio, development strategy, and data assets of large pharmaceutical companies.
- **Biotech:** Explore science-driven development, funding models, partnerships, and the role of focused data assets.
- **Biopharmaceuticals:** Situate biologics and advanced therapies within the broader industry landscape.
- **CROs (Contract Research Organizations):** Examine their role in clinical trial execution, statistical programming, data management, and operational delivery.
- **CMOs (Contract Manufacturing Organizations):** Introduce the connection between clinical development data and chemistry, manufacturing, and controls (CMC).

### Session 2: The Data Profession Landscape: Biostatistics, Data Science & AI

**Duration:** 2 hours

#### Role Profiling: Biostatistician vs. Data Scientist

| Role | Typical focus | Representative methods and outputs | Common tools and data contexts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Biostatistician** | Prespecified, protocol-driven analysis | Statistical analysis plans, survival analysis, clinical study reports (CSRs), regulatory tables and figures | SAS-dominant workflows, increasingly complemented by R; clinical trial data |
| **Data Scientist** | Exploratory, integrative, and predictive analysis | Exploratory data analysis (EDA), multi-omics integration, synthetic control arms, real-world evidence (RWE) | Python/R workflows; multimodal, observational, and real-world data |

The session will also discuss how the two roles overlap and collaborate while operating under different standards of evidence, reproducibility, and regulatory accountability.

#### AI and Machine Learning in Modern Trials

- Examine pragmatic applications of generative AI and large language models (LLMs) in clinical protocol generation, document review, and medical writing.
- Discuss machine learning for patient stratification, predictive screening, endpoint development, and biomarker discovery.
- Consider the limitations of AI in regulated settings, including validation, explainability, human oversight, bias, privacy, and data provenance.

### Session 3: Frontier Sectors & Regulatory Frameworks

**Duration:** 2 hours

#### Medical Devices and Digital Health

- **Medical devices:** Introduce Class I, II, and III classifications and the role of hardware-driven clinical validation.
- **Digital health and Software as a Medical Device (SaMD):** Discuss clinical evaluation for software-based diagnostics and AI-enabled medical products.
- **Decentralized clinical trials (DCTs):** Explore remote participation, distributed data collection, and operational implications.
- **Wearable and sensor data:** Introduce the processing and interpretation of streams from devices such as Fitbit and Pixel sensors.

#### Regulatory Frameworks and Compliance

- Compare the broad submission pathways and regulatory expectations of the **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** and the **European Medicines Agency (EMA)**.
- Introduce **CDISC** data standards, including:
  - **SDTM (Study Data Tabulation Model)** for standardized data submission.
  - **ADaM (Analysis Data Model)** for analysis-ready datasets.
- Explain the compliance pillars that govern clinical data work:
  - **GxP**, including Good Clinical Practice (GCP) and Good Laboratory Practice (GLP).
  - Validation of computerized systems.
  - Data integrity, traceability, auditability, and controlled access.

## Prerequisites

Students should have:

- A basic understanding of statistical inference.
- Familiarity with regression models.
- An interest in healthcare, clinical research, or the pharmaceutical and medical device industries.

Prior experience with SAS, R, Python, clinical trials, or regulatory submissions is helpful but not required.

## Key Terms

**Biopharma**, **Biotech**, **CRO**, **CMO**, **clinical study report (CSR)**, **CDISC**, **SDTM**, **ADaM**, **GxP**, **GCP**, **GLP**, **RWE**, **SaMD**, **DCT**, **FDA**, and **EMA**.
